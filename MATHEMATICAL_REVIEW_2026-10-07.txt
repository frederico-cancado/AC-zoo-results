1. PRECISE ABSTRACT THEOREM AND DEFINITIONS

Work in ZF. Write X<=Y for existence of an injection X->Y, and X~=Y for
existence of a bijection. Let A be NONEMPTY, and assume:
 (S) Every nonempty set X is the image of A times some ordinal.
 (W) Every ordinal-indexed family of nonempty sets has a choice function.
 (Q) Every non-well-orderable surjective image of A is bijective with A.

Non-well-orderability of A is NOT necessary for the abstract implication.
It is needed for the application witnessing not-AC. If A is well-orderable,
(S) already makes every set well-orderable, and the conclusions still hold.

The theorem proves:
 NF: every X ~= (A times kappa) disjoint-union lambda, for ordinal cardinals
     kappa and lambda;
 FB: for every nonempty SET F of sets there is a nonempty finite B subset F
     such that for every X in F there exists Y in B with Y<=X;
 FC: every sequence (X_n:n<omega) has i<j with X_i<=X_j;
 NDS: no sequence (X_n:n<omega) satisfies X_(n+1)<=X_n and not X_n<=X_(n+1)
      for every n;
 FA: every set of pairwise injection-incomparable cardinalities is finite.

FB is an existential, pointwise comparison assertion. It does not assert
that a function uniformly selects one basis member and an injection for
every member of an arbitrary, possibly non-well-orderable F. That stronger
uniformity has not been proved and is unnecessary.

2. NORMAL FORM: ALL CHOICES ARE BOUNDED

For nonempty X select one presentation s:A times eta onto X. Define
 B_i=s[A times {i}],
 S_i=B_i minus union_{j<i} B_j.
The S_i form a disjoint partition of X. Every nonempty S_i is an image of
A: for this one i, take one point of S_i as a default outside S_i. This is
only a pointwise existence proof, not a simultaneous family of defaults.

Let N be the set of indices of non-WO slices and W=eta minus N. Empty
slices belong to W. For i in N the candidate set of bijection graphs lies
in P(A times X). For i in W the candidate set of well-order relations lies
in P(X times X). Each is nonempty. Tag the candidates if desired; Replacement
forms one ordinal-indexed family of sets. Apply (W) once.

The N union is bijective with A times N. The W union is well-ordered by
slice index first, then the selected internal slice order. Replacing the
two well-orderable factors by their initial ordinals proves NF. X empty
has the representation (0,0). No rank-unbounded choice is hidden here.

3. CANONICAL PROFILE: NO CLASS CHOICE

Define kappa(X) as the least ordinal cardinal allowing some NF representation;
then lambda(X) as the least ordinal cardinal allowing that representation
with kappa(X). Nonempty definable classes of ordinals have least elements:
one existential witness bounds a nonempty set of qualifying ordinals.
The resulting minima are uniquely definable independently of that bound.

Thus Replacement forms R={(kappa(X),lambda(X)):X in F} for a set F, and
also forms the profile sequence of an actual set-valued sequence. No class
of presentations or bijections is selected. The two bijections used in a
particular comparison may be used by finite existential reasoning in ZF.

For the core theorem coordinatewise profile comparison is only SUFFICIENT.
No necessary coordinate criterion for arbitrary NF presentations is used.

4. FINITE BASIS AND THE CLEANEST COROLLARY ROUTE

Every nonempty set R of ordinal pairs has a finite coordinatewise coinitial
subset. Repeatedly select the lexicographically least remaining pair and
delete its upper cone. The selection is unique. Omega recursion can be
made total by an absorbing terminal state after the residual becomes empty.
If it never terminated, selected first coordinates would be nondecreasing,
while selected second coordinates would be strictly decreasing: any later
selected pair survived the upper-cone deletion of every earlier one. This
contradicts well-foundedness of the ordinals. Termination is therefore at
some natural-number stage. No DC is required.

Apply this to the canonical profiles of F. Finite choice (a ZF theorem)
selects one F-member for each of the finitely many basis profiles. Coordinate
inclusions and the existential NF bijections yield FB.

A concise paper can derive FC directly from FB, without another ordinal-pair
lemma. Apply FB to the range of (X_n). For each member of its finite basis,
take its least occurrence index in the sequence. Choose j above the maximum
of these finitely many indices. FB yields a basis member X_i<=X_j, with
its chosen i<j. This proof needs no choice at all beyond FB itself.

FC implies NDS: an injection X_i->X_j in a strict descending sequence,
i<j, composes with the finitely many downward injections to give
X_i->X_(i+1), contradicting strictness. Selecting/composing finitely many
maps is a ZF theorem. Howard--Tachtsis's published NDS definition is exactly
this actual set-valued-sequence formulation, not merely well-foundedness
of ordinal cardinals.

FA follows directly from FB, without invoking countable choice: a finite
coinitial basis of an antichain must contain all its cardinalities. For
Scott-cardinal coding, apply FB to the UNION of the representative sets
making up the family of Scott cardinals, rather than choosing one
representative from every cardinal. Every class in the family then equals
one of the finitely many cardinalities of the resulting basis.

Removing redundant elements from a finite basis leaves exactly all minimal
cardinalities represented by F. Every F-cardinality lies above one of them.
This does not say there are finitely many minimal SETS, nor that there is a
uniform finite bound on all antichain sizes.

7. FURTHER RESULTS: EXACT ORDER AND LATTICE AUDIT

These proofs pass, but are best stated separately under (S),(W),(Q),
non-WO(A), and the ADDITIONAL hypothesis A times 2 ~= A. The latter implies
doubling for every infinite X by NF and absorption of finite remainders.
It holds for the concrete model by the separately checked first-bit argument:
A_0={a:0 notin a}, A_1={a:0 in a} have invariant names; a proposed ordinal
enumeration of either is contradicted by copying a fresh chosen coordinate
with its assigned first bit over the support, exactly as in Proposition3.8.
Thus each half is non-WO, (Q) identifies each with A, and A=A_0 disjoint-union
A_1 gives the required doubling. This source-dependent lemma should be
written out if any lattice claim is included.

Let c(X) be the canonical least NF coefficient and w(X) the maximum ordinal
cardinal injecting into X. AC_WO proves existence of w(X): choose injections
xi->X for all xi<h(X), and well-order the union of their ranges by least
preimages in a well-ordered disjoint sum. Put rho=w(A). For kappa>0,
w(A times kappa disjoint-union beta)=max(rho,kappa,beta).

Adversarial checks of exact comparison:
 - c is monotone under injections. Slice a subset of A times c(Y) plus beta;
   its non-WO slices are copies of A, and the remaining slices have a
   well-orderable union after one bounded application of AC_WO. Their index
   set has cardinal at most c(Y).
 - If c(X)<=c(Y) and w(X)<=w(Y), separately embed X's A-part and ordinal
   remainder into Y; doubling of an infinite Y combines them. For finite Y,
   c(X)=c(Y)=0 and ordinary finite comparison applies.
Hence X<=Y iff c(X)<=c(Y) and w(X)<=w(Y). Equal (c,w) pairs characterize
bijection. This is about CANONICAL invariants, not arbitrary NF coordinates.

Set-indexed suprema and nonempty infima are coordinatewise suprema and
minima. Realization of those pairs passes:
 - c(A times c(X))=c(X), by minimality of the representation of X;
 - a nonempty minimum of canonical coefficients is attained;
 - for sigma=sup_i c(X_i), monotonicity bounds c(A times sigma) below by
   every c(X_i) and above by sigma, hence gives equality;
 - the w-coordinate formula then realizes the proposed extrema by
   A times coefficient disjoint-union the proposed w-coordinate.
All bounded/finitary cases, including zero coefficients and finite sets,
are handled in the saved proof. No family of representatives is selected.

Binary lattice distributivity therefore holds. Do NOT call the proper
class of all cardinals a complete lattice without qualification: there is
no greatest cardinal, hence no empty meet. Do NOT assert complete
distributivity involving arbitrary choice-function products; that is a
stronger and potentially AC-encoding statement. The safe formulation is:
'all set-indexed joins and all nonempty set-indexed meets exist, and binary
meet and join satisfy the distributive laws.'

The saved explicit finite antichains pass too. If kappa>rho, a representation
of A times kappa with coefficient eta<kappa would require every one of its
kappa disjoint A-copies to meet the A times eta part. AC_WO chooses distinct
such points; then kappa<=w(A times eta)=max(rho,eta)<kappa, a contradiction.
Thus c(A times kappa)=kappa above rho, and opposed increasing/decreasing
coordinate lists give antichains of every finite size.
